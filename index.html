Multi-homing and Platform Competition: Evidence from Ride-Hailing

Draft coming soon

Abstract

Platform competition in markets with strong network effects tends to tip toward a small number of platforms, raising concerns about market power. In this paper, I study the role of user multi-homing under platform competition and its welfare implications. I develop a structural model of a ride-hailing market in which competing platforms set prices strategically, and I estimate it using a unique data set covering the universe of completed trips in Nanjing, China, with driver identifiers that allow me to observe drivers’ multi-homing behavior. I then compare the estimated status quo equilibrium with counterfactual equilibria under alternative levels of multi-homing frictions and platform competition.
